What you can do

A ride for how students actually move.

Campus travel is not a series of unrelated trips. It is the same 7am lecture every weekday, the same walk back from the library at 9pm, and three friends going to the same place at the same time.

Ride types

One question: how much of the car do you want?

Every tier is the same drivers and the same campus. What changes is how many seats get sold. Prices below are live — they follow whichever campus is selected in the switcher.

Paying for it

Wallet. Top up over MTN MoMo to the campus number. The forwarder credits it without a payment provider in between.
Pay later. Low balance is not a blocker. Book anyway and settle with the driver, or top up before drop-off.
Nothing is charged up front. The wallet is debited when the driver accepts, not when you tap.
Passes and bundles. A pass covers your own seat. Book for friends and you pay their fares — said plainly, before the car, not at drop-off.

Sharing a ride

The car was already going that way.

Pooling is not a discount bolted onto a normal ride. It is the app noticing that a driver already carrying someone can pick you up without going far out of their way — and refusing the match when they can't.

1
Ama is already in a car

Heading from the Canteen to the JCR. Three seats still empty.

2
You ask for a ride nearby

Main Gate to the Library. The matcher checks what adding you would cost the trip already running.

3
The detour is measured, not guessed

If picking you up adds more delay than the campus cap allows, the match is rejected and you get your own car. Ama is not made late so that you can save GHS 2.

4
One car, two students, two fares

Same driver, one route that already made sense. The seat count on the card tells you exactly what you're joining — 1/4 means one seat taken, three free.

For drivers

Drive the campus you already know.

Traverse drivers are approved per campus, not dropped into a city-wide pool. You get requests from the stops you already serve, you see the fare before you accept, and earnings land in the same wallet the riders pay from.
