The rider app

Not a screenshot. The actual flow.

Everything in the phone runs. Pick your stops, choose a ride type, watch a driver get matched, adjust seats and see the fare change, then follow the trip to drop-off. The fare comes from the same table the product charges from — a price for each distance and party size, with a lower price for paying from the wallet or booking ahead — and every campus sets its own table, so the amounts inside the demo are illustrative rather than a price list.

    What you can do

    A ride for how students actually move.

    Campus travel is not a series of unrelated trips. It is the same 7am lecture every weekday, the same walk back from the library at 9pm, and three friends going to the same place at the same time.

      Ride types

      Standard or Solo. That is the whole choice.

      Same drivers, same campus, same stops. The only question is whether anyone else is in the car with you — share it, or take it on your own.

      What it costs

      No surge, no guessing, no meter creeping up while you sit in traffic. You see the fare before you book, and that is the fare.

      A short trip

      Up to 3 km.

      A long trip

      Over 3 km. eg. From a Diaspora hall to TF Hostel.

      GHS15

      sharing · GHS 17 with the car to yourself

      Bring people

      The price is for the trip, not a headcount. A friend riding along on a short trip takes the fare from GHS 8 to GHS 12 — for both of you, together. A third rides for the same GHS 12. Book for four and the whole car is GHS 14.

      A shared car usually takes three, so nobody is squeezed into the middle seat. On busy days, or when you book for four, it takes four.

      Pay less, easily

      Ask a couple of hours ahead, or pay from your wallet, and the fare drops — a short trip falls to GHS 7, a long one to GHS 13, and the whole car on a long run to GHS 15.

      Time is what lets us find someone already heading your way.

      Paying for it

      • Settle with the driver. Hand over the fare at drop-off, the way a taxi works. Nothing to set up, and nothing to top up first.

      • Or from the wallet. A prepaid balance in cedis, with every debit and credit on your own statement. Which top-up rail is open is a campus decision, and the app offers the wallet only where one is running.

      • Nothing is charged up front. The wallet is debited when the driver accepts, not when you tap.

      • Book ahead and pay less. Ask for a ride a couple of hours before you need it and the fare drops. The time is what lets us find someone already going your way — and a shared car is what makes any of this cheap.

      • Pay from the wallet and pay less. A fare taken from your balance costs less than one settled in cash at the end of the ride.

      • Passes and bundles. A pass covers your own seat. Book for friends and you pay their fares — said plainly, before the car, not at drop-off.

        Trust and safety

        The parts you only notice when they fail.

        A campus is small. Everyone finds out who was in whose car, which is exactly why the rules below are enforced by the product rather than left to good manners.

        No password to lose

        Signing in is a code to your email, then a code to your phone. Nothing to forget, and nothing sitting in a breach somewhere waiting to be reused.

        An SOS that leaves a record

        The button texts your trusted contact the ride details and where you are. The alert is written down as well as sent, so there is something to review afterwards instead of a memory of a text.

        Drivers get the same SOS

        A driver carries strangers at night too. They get the same button and their own emergency contact, recorded the same way. Safety on Traverse is not something one side of the car has and the other does not.

        Your week
        • Decline as often as you like. There is no acceptance rate, no score and no quiet penalty for saying no. The system has nowhere to record one.
        • Breaks are part of the rules. After a stretch on duty your campus offers a break, and the length is its policy rather than your nerve.
        • Flag-downs count too. Somebody waves you down at the gate, you start the trip in the app and it is a real trip, with a real record and a real fare.
        • You know the cut before you drive. The campus rate is set in the open and can be adjusted for an individual driver. It is a number, not a moving target.

        Off-campus pickups

        Not on campus? We will still come and get you.

        A ride does not have to start or end at a campus stop. If you stay off campus, text us and we will set your pickups up — and once you are set up, they run any day of the week. It is the arranging that happens at weekends, so text us on a Saturday with where you are and the days you need.

        How an off-campus pickup works

        • The pickups themselves run any day. Once you are set up, your ride can be whatever day you need it — weekday mornings included.

        • The arranging happens at weekends, Saturday for preference. That is when we plan the week’s off-campus runs and work out who can share a car. Text us then, not on the day you need the ride.

        • Text, do not call. WhatsApp or SMS, either is fine — but it has to be in writing so there is a record of who asked for what. You do need SMS working on your phone, because that is where your confirmation comes.

        • GHS 18 a seat, to anywhere on campus. One price from your place to wherever you are actually going — your hall, your department, the library. There is nothing to add on when you get through the gate.

        • Three of you, minimum. Not a discount rule — a car has three seats on an ordinary day, and an off-campus run only works if it leaves full. On campus a driver can set off with one person because others get on along the way; out here nobody does.

        • Have the balance ready before the day. Your in-app balance has to cover your own seat and every seat you booked for someone else. Nothing is taken when you book — the fare comes out when the driver picks you up.

        • You will hear from us twice, at most. Once when your ride is confirmed — and again at least an hour before your pickup time if it turns out we cannot make it. No news after the confirmation means the car is coming.

        • A seat is not guaranteed. Filling the car is our job, not yours. If we cannot find three people going your way on the day you asked for, the run does not happen — we will tell you in good time and offer you the next one.
